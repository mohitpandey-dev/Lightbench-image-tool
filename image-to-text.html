Image to text (OCR)

Pull the text out of a photo, scan or document. Works best with clear, well-lit, printed text.

The first time you recognise text, your browser downloads a small recognition engine and language file, so it needs an internet connection. After that it is cached for next time.

Tips for better results: use a sharp, well-lit photo; keep the text roughly horizontal; and crop out anything you do not need read.

How to use Image to text (OCR)

  1. Add a photo, scan or screenshot that contains text.
  2. Wait while the text is recognised in your browser.
  3. Read through the result and correct any mistakes.
  4. Copy the text or save it.

Why use this tool

Optical character recognition, or OCR, reads the letters in a picture and turns them into text you can search, copy and edit. It saves you retyping a printed page, a receipt, a sign or a quote. Because the work happens in your browser, private documents stay on your device.

Tips for best results

  • Use a sharp, well-lit picture taken straight on. Shadows and angles cause errors.
  • Crop to just the text. Pictures and borders can confuse the reader.
  • Always proofread. Letters like O and 0, or l and 1, are easy to mix up.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is OCR?

Clear printed text is usually read well. Blurry, tilted or decorative text can contain mistakes.

Can it read handwriting?

Handwriting is much harder. Try the handwriting tool and expect to correct the result.

Is my image sent to a server?

No. Recognition runs on your device.
